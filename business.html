The Business Reality

Data is not just text in a database. It is the physical reality of your business.

I don't just audit data. I understand how your business transacts. I look at the intersection of data mechanics, financial leaks, and sales strategy. Here are the three lenses I look through when I audit your systems:

1. The Mechanics

Data represents physical items.

Take a simple Unit of Measure. Standard tools see "CA" and "CS" as valid two-character codes. I know that CA is Case and CS is Cassette. If your ERP doesn't catch this, you order a single cassette when you meant to order a full case. The invoice fails, and the supply chain breaks.

2. The Financials

Data hides the money you are losing.

A salesperson sells a $1.00 part for $1.33 and thinks they made a 25% margin. They didn't. The G/L is absorbing a 27% inventory carrying cost on that part because it sat on a shelf for a year. I trace the data down to the General Ledger to find the real financial leaks.

3. The Strategy

Data reveals the blind spots.

I audited a distributor in a heavy manufacturing town where a single automation vendor was 55% of sales. I asked where the cutting tools were. They had none. A manufacturing plant chews through saw blades. Their data was clean, but their strategy was blind.

Where the Circles Overlap

When you look at the mechanics, the financials, and the strategy together, you start to see the structural risks hiding in plain sight.

The Activity KPI Trap: A distributor had massive conversions of product lines per quarter, but overall sales were flat. Salespeople were swapping one line for another just to hit an activity KPI. It met the KPI, but created orphaned inventory that silently accumulated 27% in carrying costs. A dashboard showed a successful quarter. I saw the hidden financial leak.

Sometimes, finding the leak means walking the warehouse floor. I once found a $75 part bought in 1983 sitting on a shelf in 2008. When I dug into the data, I realized it wasn't an isolated incident. On a $1.2 million inventory, a massive percentage was dead stock. The carrying costs were draining the company nearly $40,000 a year. I told the president to write it off. He yelled at me until I showed him the math.

Sometimes, the best code is no code. You don't need a massive cloud application to find dead inventory. You just need to walk the floor, look at the data, and know how to read the General Ledger.

When I look at a distributor's sales mix, I don't get excited when one vendor is dominating. I get nervous. If 55% of your revenue comes from one vendor, you have zero negotiating leverage. If that vendor changes their pricing, restricts supply, or decides to sell direct, your business is underwater.

The 'Three Big Accounts' Syndrome

I see the same structural risk in outside sales territories. If a salesperson has three massive accounts and zero mid-tier accounts, their book of business looks incredibly healthy on a dashboard. But if they lose just one of those accounts, their entire territory is wiped out overnight. Standard reporting won't catch this. I look at the data and see critical risk.

I don't just clean your data. I look at the structural risk hiding inside it, and I tell you what your data is forgetting. To do that, I don't need the cloud. I need a database that runs as fast as I can think.

Why SQLite is the Best Choice

When people hear "SQLite," they often assume it's just for mobile apps or quick prototypes. They think you need a dedicated SQL Server or a cloud-based PostgreSQL instance to run a real business. SQLite is the most widely deployed database engine in the world. It's built into every smartphone, every web browser, and most operating systems. It is incredibly stable, ACID-compliant, and fast. For 90% of the data problems small and mid-sized businesses face, a cloud database is overkill.

The Cloud Database Tax

A managed SQL Server or cloud Postgres instance will cost you hundreds (or thousands) of dollars a month. You pay for the server uptime, the network latency, and the DBA to manage it.

If your internet goes down, your data goes down with it.

The SQLite Advantage

SQLite is a single file on your hard drive. There is no server to configure, no network latency, and zero hosting costs.

It can handle millions of rows locally. It is fully secure because it physically never leaves your machine.

I don't pick the tool because it's trendy. I pick it because it's the right tool for the job. If you have a local-first workflow, SQLite will outperform a cloud database every single time.
